## Kim and Park (2024)
[Journal of Big Data11,11](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40537-023-00867-5), Seongeun Kim and Eunil Park, “Prediction of flight departure delays caused by weather conditions adopting data-driven approaches”. CC BY4.0.

**Method/data:** DT,RF,SVM,KNN,LR,XGB,LSTM compared for ICN,JFK,MDW. Weather shifted2/4/8/16/24/48hours, plus historical flight outcomes. Table4:1,569,879/773,347/404,507flights, only7,850/9,417/5,562weather-delayed cases. ICN threshold explicitly exceeds one hour; US selection is described analogously with weather causes, but simultaneous BTS cause-code handling is unclear.
**Results:** Normal flights randomly undersampled1:1 before splitting. Best2-hour accuracy .749 ICN/RF,.852 JFK/LSTM,.785 MDW/LSTM;48-hour RF .748/.846/.772(Tables10–12). JFK/LSTM2h delayed-class precision .876,recall .822,F1 .848. These are artificially balanced classification results, not calibrated operating probabilities.
**Checks/our interpretation:** Chronological test not clearly documented. Text says80:20 then80:20 but gives67:13:20; arithmetic is64:16:20. Table7 also differs from the stated balancing counts. Table6 labels temperatures reaching103as Celsius and pressure near29.3as hPa: possible unresolved unit errors. Table10 has an inconsistent DT48h cell, unsuitable as a superiority claim. Earlier observed weather does not evaluate archived as-issued weather forecasts. Weather features do not causally allocate all delay causes.
**Sources:** References5/44 BTS,40 KMA AMOS,41 New York weather URL actually KLGA despite JFK flights,42 KMDW. Station mapping unresolved. Data on request. Replication needs exact BTS filters,label threshold,timezones/stations,split indices.
**FRA:** Model/horizon comparison inspiration; no validation of07/25,runway18 or calibrated48h operating probabilities.

## Yuan, Kong and Chen (2025)
[Applied Sciences15,8604](https://doi.org/10.3390/app15158604), “Delay Prediction Through Multi-Channel Traffic and Weather Scene Image: A Deep Learning-Based Method”. CC BY4.0.
**Method:** Guangzhou Baiyun2019,hourly ADS-B traffic and convective-weather images. Fuzzy C-Means derives two airport disruption states from mean departure delay and on-time rate, approximately below/above50minutes. Not an individual weather-delayed-flight target. CNN uses traffic volume,altitude,speed and three weather levels;145channel combinations and grid comparisons.70:30split, chronology unspecified; resampling explicitly training-only.
**Results:** Table3 eight-channel accuracy90.82%. Table4 ROS-CNN accuracy .831,severe-class precision .28,recall .78,F1 .41; RF accuracy .898,severe recall .23/F1 .25. Table5,25×25grid:91.61%accuracy,severe recall .42(73/172),precision .72,F1 .53.
**Checks/our interpretation:** No clear forecast timestamp or input-to-target lead time; concurrent scene classification is not forecasting future delay. Independent validation for selecting145variants is unclear: potential selection bias, not proven leakage. Table4 test N2284 versus Table5 N1525 is unexplained. Majority baselines calculated from these tables are92.34%and88.72%; accuracy alone hides minority difficulty. Different variants must not be combined. Data on request.
**FRA:** Possible spatial convection features; no48h,runway-direction or safety evidence.

## Liu et al. (2024), preprint
[arXiv:2405.08293v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08293), “Airport Delay Prediction with Temporal Fusion Transformers”. No independently verified journal publication.
**Method/results:** Top30US airports,2016,1,054,080quarter-hour observations; ASPM and ISD2330weather stations. TFT2h lookback,4h horizon; smoothed mean arrival delay, not weather-cause label. First11.5months train/validation,last15days test(pp.3–4). Airport MAE roughly5–12minutes; no comparable baseline table,horizon-specific performance or verified interval calibration.
**Checks/our interpretation:** Temporal holdout is useful but only a short winter period. TableI treats future weather/traffic as known; described ISD data are observations. Use of genuinely available issued forecasts is unverified. Three-adjacent-quarter smoothing may be causal or centred; unspecified. Both issues require future-information checks. Authors acknowledge missing Traffic Management Initiatives. Keywords are unrelated; a mentioned appendix is absent from the five-page file.
**Sources:** Reference[4]led to Vandal2018. Reference[17]led to original Lim TFT; PDF saved. Reference[5], supposedly Wang/Tien/Chou “Multi-airport delay prediction with transformers”, links to that same Lim paper(arXiv:1912.09363), not the stated title. Bibliographic error, not evidence of another airport study.
**FRA:** Multi-horizon architecture idea; no48h,07/25or runway18 empirical validation.

## Chain A: Liu[4] → Vandal → uncertainty/weather ablation
Thomas Vandal,Max Livingston,Camen Piho,Sam Zimmerman, “Prediction and uncertainty quantification of daily airport flight delays”, [PMLR82:45–51,2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v82/vandal18a.html). Full PDF read; PDF header2017, bibliographic publication year2018per PMLR.
123US airports,July2012–July2016; daily The Weather Company observations and proprietary flight feeds. Wind/gusts,temperature,pressure,visibility,rain/snow,flight counts,previous delay,calendar features. Variational LSTM,MC-dropout .25,30samples,last20%chronological test. Table1 “MAE” means **Median Absolute Error**:1/3/5/7day values5.84/6.60/7.22/7.71minutes; RMSE10.64/11.41/12.03/12.56. Weather ablation improves RMSE about one minute; rare extremes are underestimated(pp.4–6).
**Learning:** Evaluate error,uncertainty,calibration and extreme days separately. Ablation tests incremental weather information more directly than feature importance. Proprietary data prevent open replication; whether each horizon used genuinely available forecasts rather than historical weather is unclear. No causal allocation of all delays or FRA-transfer evidence.

## Chain B: Zinkhan → original DWD ICON-D2 documentation
Dirk Zinkhan,Anneliesa Greisbach,Björn Zurmaar,Christina Klüver,Jürgen Klüver, “Intrinsic Explainable Self-Enforcing Networks Using the ICON-D2-Ensemble Prediction System for Runway Configurations”, [Engineering Proceedings39,41(2023)](https://doi.org/10.3390/engproc2023039041),conference paper,CC BY4.0.
**Method/results:** Direct FRA07/25application. Parallel wind at11glidepath/ground positions to~5000m,five quantiles each,55features. Expert meteorological/ATC weights/reference types; Self-Enforcing Network,normalized features,learning rate .5,three iterations. Illustrative15h case recommends25switch at08UTC; actual02:49UTC decision starts daytime operation in25at04UTC because weak winds permit it and avoid a later switch. Different forecast runs shift recommendations.
**Our interpretation:** No quantified test sample,independent temporal backtest,accuracy/calibration table. Activation is not calibrated probability. No separate runway18availability evaluation. Section3says COSMO-DE-EPS while title/method say ICON-D2; version ambiguity requires resolution. Spatial wind,expert preferences and switch timing are useful concepts.
**Original[32]:** Cited [DWD URL](https://www.dwd.de/SharedDocs/downloads/DE/modelldokumentationen/nwv/icon_d2/icon_d2_dbbeschr_aktuell.pdf?view=nasPublication&nn=13934) now serves “DWD Database Reference for the Global and Regional ICON and ICON-EPS Forecasting System”,v2.5.6(2026),198pages,**not frozen2023version**. VMAX_10M is an hourly maximum,not instantaneous wind; U_10M_AV/V_10M_AV are10minute mean components. Products distinguish mean/spread,quantiles,exceedance probabilities.
**Further learning:** Retain member,initialization,valid time,vertical level,units,statistic interval. Derive runway-relative components; gust maxima are not mean wind. Member paths preserve temporal dependence that separate quantile curves can lose. Historical as-issued availability needs separate checking. Ensemble fractions alone do not justify clearance.

Tejas G.Puranik,Milad Memarzadeh,Krishna M.Kalyanam, “Predicting airport runway configurations for decision-support using supervised learning”,DASC2023,[NASA original](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230009416),DOI10.1109/DASC58513.2023.10311186. Nine pages fully read.

METAR and latest already available LAMP forecasts next2h; scheduled next-hour demand,capacity,time,go-arounds. Actual flight counts/taxi times excluded because unavailable in time(pp.3–5,TableII). Labels derived from observed flights because ASPM configuration reports can be unreliable.
Random80:20,stratified5fold-CV. RF TableIV/p.6: CLT accuracy93.94%/weightedF1 93.96%; DEN73.49%/74.05%. Most-frequent historical controller-action baseline81.6%/53.7%. Defined obvious-wind-decision violations below .07%/.7%(p.7): specific plausibility metric,not general safety proof.

**Data source followed:** [NOAA LAMP](https://vlab.noaa.gov/web/mdl/lamp),[v2.7.0definition](https://vlab.noaa.gov/web/mdl/lamp-card-2.7.0),[NCEP services](https://vlab.noaa.gov/web/mdl/lamp-nws-webservices). ## General-cause and FRA implications
No universal weather-delay percentage follows. Targets differ:selected weather label,total mean delay,airport disruption class,human runway configuration. Feature importance is not causality. BTS codes,weather events,propagation,capacity decisions are different levels.
FRA07/25,runway18availability,next switch and delay are separate targets. Project wind limits originate in the official brief,not a validation by these papers. Research checks include chronological/event splits,persistence/rule/wind baselines,separate rare closures/switches,calibration by horizon,and issue-time forecasts instead of retrospective weather. These are research suggestions,not operational recommendations. No literature model was locally replicated. The separate BTS descriptive analysis does not validate FRA outputs.
