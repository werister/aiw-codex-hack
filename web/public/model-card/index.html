Model card · Frankfurt operating direction and runway 18

Plain-language German explanation: ML_VERBESSERUNGEN.md.

Model and intended use

walk-forward-v6 predicts operating direction 25/07 and inferred runway-18 weather usability at each of the next 48 hourly targets. It also reports the first predicted direction change and a certainty score, 100 × min(|2p_OD25−1|, |2p_R18−1|). This margin is not a calibrated probability. The displayed change window is a probability-band heuristic, not a calibrated event-time interval.

The system supports planning in the FraAlliance challenge. DFS decides the actual operating direction; these forecasts are not operational clearances.

Two scikit-learn histogram gradient-boosted classifiers use 45 features: recorded state, METAR wind/trends, forecast wind, rules, separate weather models, pressure differences, local hour, season and lead. OD is blended with persistence by horizon and regime; R18 uses horizon-only weights. Each fold selects weights by Brier score on its preceding 60-day validation block, with labels purged across the inner boundary. Sparse or closure-free R18 validation and unknown current R18 state use the raw classifier. The model architecture remains unchanged.

Inputs cover 2024 onward for weather forecasts; longer METAR/DFLD history supports state and label checks. Archived runs use day 1 for leads 1–18, day 2 for 19–42, and day 3 for 43–48, with a six-hour publication proxy. Every explicit issuance and retrieval timestamp must also be no later than issue time. Observed positive tailwind duration seeds both rule paths and the CLI fallback.

Live issues are rounded down to the hour. A newly fetched snapshot after that timestamp is ineligible; the existing dated-run fallback uses a twelve-hour publication proxy. Neither buffer proves actual historical availability. DFLD publication delay and summer-clock alignment remain unverified. Wind scales are existing fixed constants documented as fitted on 2024; this change neither refits them per fold nor independently reconstructs that calibration.

Independent data verification (Kai Detmers)

Offline checks, all exit 0 (resources/ml-depth/verification.md): - DFLD labels: all 76,588 hourly labels are rebuilt exactly from 154,752 half-hour values; the 788 excluded hours and 2,880 raw HTML samples match. - METAR: 46/46 raw file hashes match, with 32,156 wind groups and zero normalisation deviations. - Forecast wind: 144,072 values from 22 monthly responses re-checked. 72 hourly ICON vectors match their assigned runs; the six-hour publication buffer is still an assumption. - TAF: the IEM archive returned zero EDDF rows, so TAF is not used (resources/ml-support/taf/findings.md). - Wind bias: ICON surface wind runs about 3.3 kt below the airport sensor, stable across 22 months (7-day blocks −3.49 to −3.15 kt; against the DWD station −3.07 kt). That is why forecast wind is scaled to airport level.

Further limits: - Change hours: 770 of the 1,681 detectable direction changes fall in transition hours that the stable-hour accuracy excludes, so changes are scored on the half-hour data instead. - Runway 18 and variable wind: 4,289 VRB reports and 24 direction ranges make the 15 kt rule ambiguous. VRB hours with wind stay unlabelled (2,067 DFLD hours, 2024-12 to 2026-10). Labelling them usable is planned, not applied. - Threshold: Fraport's public page describes a lower tailwind threshold for direction changes than the official brief's 10 kt or more. We use the brief's.

Changes and rare runway-18 cases

First-change timing is direction-sensitive and uses actual half-hour state at issue time. It scores 750 overlapping change windows: 27.2% within ±1 h and 52.5% within ±3 h. False-alarm rate is 14.7% over 593 no-change windows, not the fraction of warnings that were false. 89 incomplete or invalid windows are excluded.

The separate unique-event view matches 115 of 193 actual transitions within ±3 h: recall 59.6%, deduplicated precision 45.5%, 138 false-warning clusters across 358.5 covered days. Matching requires the warning to have been issued before the event. A minimum-error assignment summarizes matched warning lead; it does not measure earliest useful preparation time.

R18 outcomes are overlapping positive issue windows, not unique closure episodes. Every target and compared prediction within the horizon must be known. Unknown or variable METAR direction stays unlabelled. Method cells show hits / false alarms:

Prior work and next experiments

  • Zinkhan et al. (2023), DWD Offenbach with a university group: "Intrinsic Explainable Self-Enforcing Networks Using the ICON-D2-Ensemble Prediction System for Runway Configurations", Engineering Proceedings 39, 41 (CC BY). This is related work, not a benchmark: it shows one illustrative 15 h case at FRA with no backtest score and no calibration, and its text uses COSMO-DE-EPS although the title names ICON-D2. Review: Luca Schultz, weather database/models/NOTES.md (commit 0c8f4da on his branch).
  • Puranik et al. (2023), NASA Ames: "Predicting airport runway configurations for decision-support using supervised learning". This is a method reference for METAR-derived features. Their 93.9% (CLT) and 73.5% (DEN) predict the configuration only 15 minutes ahead, on a random 80:20 split, against baselines of 81.6% and 53.7%. That is not comparable to our 6–48 h chronological walk-forward.

Next (not yet tested): - ICON-D2 ensemble spread as a candidate certainty feature. Zinkhan et al. motivate it, but Open-Meteo has no ensemble archive before 29 Jul 2026, so it cannot support this twelve-month comparison. - A wind correction conditional on direction and regime, for runway 18. - No cost per direction change is claimed: that needs FRA operations data.
