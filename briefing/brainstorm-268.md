# Team brainstorm, 30 Oct about 11:30 (recording 268)

Recorded at the table by Jayden, mostly in German. The bullets come first, then the full transcript, unedited.

## Bullet points (what we decided and want)

**Product and UI**
- **The head agent builds the frontend.** Don't overload it; show what the model does. Simple yes/no states, with a click to drill into details.
- **Use FraAlliance's design (their CI and design system)** for the dashboard.
- **Simulate the tower/terminal software:** a realistic demo of the ops screen where you see what lands and departs where, and which runway direction is active.
- **Persona: the people in the tower.** They work under stress every day, so the interface must be dead simple to use and to demo.
- **Optional 3D animation for the demo** (Blender, or GPT-6 Astra building it). One separate agent can try it; it's only a nice extra.

**Model and evaluation**
- **Show in-sample vs out-of-sample:** we trained on these data, and on unseen data we reach X accuracy. Pick the right test framework: walk-forward vs cross validation, likely walk-forward because it's a time series.
- **Dashboard section for model quality:** training sets, the data used, out-of-sample accuracy (official brief: 1, 6 and 24 h).
- **Confidence score** on every forecast.
- Idea: delays matter. If a flight is often late, the wind can turn in that extra hour, so the forecast should carry that uncertainty.
- **Delay data could be valuable:** it can show whether a past delay came from a wind or runway change, or from something else. That's a nice to have.
- **Other airports for more training data** (Munich, Paris, Zurich, Lyon, Frankfurt-Hahn, also to see how wind fronts move): probably too much for today.

**Data and compute**
- Flight and wind data is light. It can train on Jayden's home PCs (GPU), or on Google Colab (Luca's suggestion).
- Wind APIs: **Windy API** came up (needs an account and key). Look for others too, preferring keyless ones.

**Ops background (why it matters)**
- Goal: know 48 h ahead which runway direction is needed when, to plan capacity, instead of "the wind turns, now we have to move 10 aircraft", which causes delays.
- Moving aircraft also moves **staff and crews**, so knock-on effects follow. Each aircraft has a maximum time it can be shifted before chain effects start.
- **The priority is always getting the aircraft off the ground.**

**How we work**
- **Spec driven with OpenSpec**: plan first, so every agent sees what's planned. Split into work packages. No GitHub issues.
- **Commit immediately and always, even if unfinished. Rebase directly.** Constantly check what the others did. Agents resolve conflicts.
- Everyone builds everything; AI can't cleanly split frontend vs backend by person.
- Tools: our agents and Codex. Jayden logs in with the team Codex account (statworx workspace, **GPT-6 Astra is unlimited**), so others can use it too.
- Target: done building well before 17:00.
